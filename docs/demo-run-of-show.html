PitWall 24H, shown as a race

A guided demo built on a real recorded timing session — the Asian Le Mans Series feed, 24 cars, 2 h 08 of racing with two neutralisations. The recording drives the screens; you drive the story, and everything pauses when you do.

4 team cars: #26 · #34 · #45 · #96 (LMP2) replay: 22 Aug  22:15:22  ·  127:59  ·  878 kB ~18 min + Q&A

Your three pause levers

Nothing in this demo runs away from you. The strategy side only moves when you click — the two things that move on their own each have a brake:

The timing feed ⏸ PAUSE in SETTINGS → REPLAYS freezes the replay — standings, tracker, session clock. PLAY resumes; the speed box (1–300×) and the slider jump the story.
The fuel burn Press RED (flag buttons, any screen): burn stops race-wide — which is itself a feature to show. Press it again to go racing.
Everything, back to the start SETTINGS → BACKUPS → RESTORE on your snapshot rewinds the whole race state. Your reset button between rehearsals — or mid-meeting.

Before the meeting 15:30

  1. Close every running copy of the app.
  2. Terminal in the project folder: npm start → START PIT WALL.
  3. Second terminal: node test/demo-setup.mjs — wait for the DEMO READY block. It names the four cars, links them to the feed, fills the driver tables, and leaves the race un-started. (If the wall's top bar shows a port other than 8484, pass it: node test/demo-setup.mjs <port>.)
  4. Double-click test\demo-station.cmd → pick Car 1, leave the address empty → START STATION. The badge top-right must read connected and the header #26 G-Drive Racing By Algarve.
  5. On the wall: ⚙ SETTINGS → REPLAYS → open the big entry 22/08 22:15:22 · 127:59 · 878 kB (the list dates each recording by when it ended), OK the confirmation. Press PLAY, let it run to about 1:00 so the board fills with the 24-car field, then ⏸ PAUSE.
  6. ⚙ SETTINGS → BACKUPS → SAVE SNAPSHOT NOW — this is the state every reset returns to.
  7. Windows: pit wall on the screen you share; the station one Alt-Tab away (or side by side). This page on a phone or second screen.

Worth one rehearsal — run scenes 3–6 once before the meeting, then RESTORE the snapshot. Ten minutes, and the stop flow will be in your fingers.

SCENE 1

The one-app pitch

1 minreplay ⏸ 1:00

Say

One app on five PCs. Four engineers each run a station for their car; the fifth PC is the pit wall — the big board the mechanics read. The wall hosts the data; stations connect over the local network. Every change is written to disk the moment it happens, so a crash mid-race loses nothing.

What's frozen on screen is a recorded timing session being re-simulated through the app's own engine — the same path a live feed takes on race day.

SCENE 5

A yellow drops

4 minreplay ▸ 9:00

Do

  • Point at the green-lap strip first: IF A FLAG DROPS NOW — what a Code 60 and a Safety Car would each be worth on this lap, computed in advance.
  • Drag the replay to 9:00, PLAY at 1×. At 9:10 the feed's chip flashes FCY.
  • Press the FCY button. Banner on every screen; each car's verdict appears: STAY OUT — box now adds a stop.
  • On the station, time-travel the car 45 minutes into its stint with the two correction controls: Correct total laps → 18 → SET (stint laps and tyre mileage jump with it), then Correct fuel reading → 18 → SET. The low-fuel banner comes up amber, the window is OPEN, and the verdict flips: BOX NOW — saves N s · fill to X L.

Say The corrections are race-day tools — a real reading beats an estimate any time — which is why they double as the demo's fast-forward: the app happily accepts that 45 minutes just happened. With a live feed all four cars switch automatically the moment the flag flies — the button is the fallback when the feed is late, and whoever sees the boards first makes the call for every screen. A stop costs the same seconds under any flag; what changes is how much track those seconds cost you. Under Code 60 the field crawls, so the stop is discounted — the app prices it per car, tyres and driver change included, and it prices the Safety Car differently because the queue runs faster. That difference is where 24-hour races are won.

If something goes sideways

  • Numbers look wrong / clicked yourself into a corner — SETTINGS → BACKUPS → RESTORE the snapshot. Every screen resets in a second. Say it's a feature — because it is.
  • Replay list is empty — REFRESH LIST; make sure you're on the pit wall's settings, not the station's.
  • Station badge not connected — station ⚙ SETTINGS → CONNECTION → APPLY & RECONNECT (address empty, port 8484).
  • demo-setup says timeout — the wall isn't started or is on another port; the wall's top bar shows the port to pass.
  • Nuclear option — close both copies, npm start, START PIT WALL, BACKUPS → RESTORE, reopen the replay, relaunch the station. Under two minutes.

Likely questions, one-line answers

What happens when the timing feed dies mid-race?
The race keeps running on the app's own clock, manual lap logging reappears by itself, and when the feed returns the laps run during the outage are put back on the counters.
And if the pit wall PC dies?
State is written to disk on every change plus timestamped snapshots every five minutes; restart, restore, and the stations reconnect on their own.
Which timing feeds does it speak?
getraceresults.com pages, Al Kamel pages, and the paid TeamStream team feed directly — and every live session is recorded for replay, which is what you watched today.
Other circuits than Zolder?
Track length, pit lane, sectors are event settings typed in once on the wall; Zolder's official figures are just the defaults.
How does it get to five PCs?
A Windows installer with auto-update from releases — every PC picks new versions up on launch.
What does it cost to run?
It's ours, it runs on the garage LAN, and nothing leaves the building except the connection to the timekeeper's feed.
